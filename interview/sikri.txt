-> A typical Agile project can look like this end to end: - 
   Business Idea -> Requirements / Product Backlog -> Epic → User Stories → Tasks -> Sprint Planning -> 
   Development -> Code Review -> CI/CD + Testing -> QA / UAT -> Sprint Review -> Release -> Retrospective -> Next Sprint

  - The Product Owner breaks the requirement into smaller pieces. Priority of the tickets decided by the product owner
  - An Epic is a large business capability. A user story describes functionality from the user's perspective.. A Task is a technical step to complete a User Story.
  - All the stories/features that need to be built go into the Product Backlog.
  - Backlog Grooming :: Before a sprint, the team discusses upcoming stories.This is often called Backlog Refinement or Backlog Grooming.
  - Estimation :: The development team estimates the complexity of stories. A common Agile technique is Story Points.
  - For story point :: A team might use: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21. This is commonly based on the Fibonacci sequence. 
  - Sprint Planning :: The team looks at the prioritized backlog and decides what can realistically be completed during the sprint.
  - Sprint Review :: At the end of the sprint, the team demonstrates the completed functionality to stakeholders.
  - Sprint Retrospective :: After the sprint, the team discusses how the team worked, rather than what product functionality was delivered.
    - What went well? , What didn't go well? , What should we improve?
  - Daily Scrum / Stand-up 
    - What did I do yesterday? , What will I do today? , Do I have any blockers?

-> The important Agile roles (Scrum roles)
    - Product Owner :: Responsible for product value and backlog prioritization.
    - Scrum Master :: Helps the team follow Scrum practices and removes organizational/process impediments.
    - Developers :: Responsible for building the product increment.
    - Stakeholders :: Anyone with an interest in the product.
=> Difference between Agile and scrum 
    - Agile is a mindset based on iterative development, continuous feedback, customer collaboration, and responding to change. Scrum is a framework 
      that implements Agile principles using defined roles, events, artifacts, and time-boxed sprints.
=> You can explain an Agile project in one flow:
    - Business requirements are converted into epics and user stories, which are prioritized in the product backlog. During refinement, the team 
      clarifies requirements and estimates the stories. In sprint planning, the team selects stories for the sprint and breaks them into technical 
      tasks. Developers implement the stories, write tests, create pull requests, and go through code review and CI/CD. QA validates the functionality 
      against acceptance criteria. Completed work is demonstrated during the sprint review, and the team discusses process improvements during the 
      retrospective. Feedback and new requirements go back into the backlog, and the cycle repeats until the product is delivered.
