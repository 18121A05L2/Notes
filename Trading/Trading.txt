1. Momentum Indicators 
Definition: These measure the speed and magnitude of recent price changes to assess the strength and potential continuation of a trend.
Example (MACD): Uses histogram bars where green indicates bullish momentum and red indicates bearish momentum. Darker shades show increasing momentum, while lighter shades show a decline.

2. Volume Indicators 
Definition: These analyze the volume of trades to confirm the strength and sustainability of price movements.
Example (Hawkeye Volume): Uses color-coded bars and a moving average; green bars show bullish volume, red show bearish, and gray indicate neutrality or lack of volume.

3. Divergence Indicators 
Definition: Used to identify when the price trend moves in a different direction than an oscillator, signaling potential reversals.
Example (Divergence Histogram): Aggregates data from multiple indicators to plot green (bullish) or red (bearish) divergence bars.

4. Overextension Indicators 
Definition: Identify when prices have moved too far too fast, indicating overbought or oversold conditions.
Example (Money Flow Index): Values above 80 suggest overbought conditions, while values below 20 suggest oversold conditions.

5. Buy/Sell Indicators 
Definition: Tools that use various price action methodologies to provide clear, actionable signals for entering or exiting trades.
Example (Market Beacon): Provides direct "long" or "short" signals on the chart.

6. Trend Indicators 
Definition: Used to identify the direction and strength of a trend to determine potential continuation or reversal.
Example (Trend Strength Signals): Uses a "Trend Cloud" (angled up for bullish, down for bearish) and a strength gauge to show how strong the current move is.

7. Support and Resistance Indicators 
Definition: Identify price levels where the market tends to find support (stopping a fall) or resistance (stopping a rise).
Example (Chart Prime S&R): Uses pivot points and volume to create green "support" boxes and red "resistance" boxes.

8. Smart Money Concepts (SMC) Indicators 
Definition: Focus on the behavior of institutional investors and large traders to gauge market sentiment.
Example (Lux Algo SMC): An all-in-one tool displaying market structures like Order Blocks, Change of Character (CHoCH), and Break of Structure (BOS).

9. Volatility Indicators 
Definition: Measure the rate of price fluctuations over time to assess risk and reward.
Example (Average True Range - ATR): Measures the average range between highs and lows. High ATR values suggest wider stop-losses are needed.

10. Candlestick Pattern Indicators 
Definition: Use specific formations of individual candlesticks (like hammers or engulfing bars) to predict future price moves.
Example (All Candlestick Patterns): Automatically labels patterns like Dojis or Morning Stars on the chart.

11. Chart Pattern Indicators 
Definition: Focus on larger price formations (rather than single candles) that signal future movements.
Example (Auto Chart Pattern): Identifies geometric shapes such as wedges, triangles, and channels.

12. Channel Indicators 
Definition: Create "envelopes" around price movements to identify trends and overbought/oversold levels.
Example (Keltner Channels): Consists of upper, middle, and lower lines; price breakthroughs of the outer bands indicate trend strength or potential exhaustion.

13. Retracement Indicators 
Definition: Identify potential support and resistance levels based on horizontal lines following a significant price move.
Example (Fibonacci Retracement): Uses mathematical ratios (like 0.382) to predict where a price pullback might end and reverse.
